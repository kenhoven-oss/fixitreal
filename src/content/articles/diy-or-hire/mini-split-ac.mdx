A mini-split (also called a split unit or ductless AC) is three jobs in one box: mounting, refrigerant and electrical. A capable homeowner can do the first. The second depends on which kind of system you buy, and the law around it is less clear than the product listings suggest. The third belongs to an electrician.

<KeyTakeaway title="The short answer">
A conventional mini-split is not a DIY install: connecting it means handling refrigerant, which requires EPA certification. A pre-charged quick-connect kit is built for homeowners, but EPA's own guidance says connecting pre-charged lines also requires certification. Do the mounting yourself, hire an electrician for the wiring, and decide the refrigerant step with both facts in front of you.
</KeyTakeaway>

## The three parts of the job

| Part | Who can do it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mounting the indoor unit, drilling the wall, setting the outdoor unit, routing the lines and drain | Homeowner | Carpentry and layout; no refrigerant or live wiring |
| Refrigerant connection on a conventional unit | EPA-certified technician | Flaring, vacuum and charging are regulated refrigerant handling |
| Refrigerant connection on a pre-charged quick-connect kit | Disputed; see below | Manufacturer says homeowner; EPA guidance says certification |
| Dedicated circuit, disconnect and power connection | Licensed electrician | High-voltage work, usually permitted |

## What the EPA says, and what the kit makers say

Section 608 of the Clean Air Act requires certification for work that could release refrigerant. EPA's published questions and answers put the mini-split case directly: asked whether certification is needed to install one, the agency answers yes, and it lists connecting or disconnecting hoses or pre-charged lines among the work that requires it. EPA also restricts refrigerant sales to certified technicians or their employers.

Makers of pre-charged kits take a different position. Their line sets arrive factory-sealed, with fittings that open only as they are tightened together, so the installer never handles refrigerant, and they sell the systems as needing no vacuum pump, gauges or HVAC license.

Two things to know about that gap:

- The EPA page was archived in January 2021 and speaks about R-410A systems. It does not mention quick-connect fittings, and it predates the R-454B kits now on sale.
- Neither side's statement settles it for you. If you want no doubt, do the mounting yourself and pay a certified technician to make the connections. Ask before you buy, because some technicians will not work on owner-supplied equipment.

For a conventional mini-split with flared copper lines there is no gap. That install is certified work.

## What you can do yourself

- **Choose the locations.** Indoor unit high on the wall with clear airflow; outdoor unit on a level base with the clearances the manual gives.
- **Mount the indoor bracket and drill the wall.** The hole slopes slightly down toward the outside so condensate drains out, not in.
- **Set the outdoor unit.** A ground pad or a [condenser wall bracket](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=mini+split+condenser+wall+bracket&tag=fixitreal-20) keeps it level and off the soil.
- **Route the line set and drain.** Do not kink the lines, and never cut or shorten a pre-charged set; coil the excess as the manual shows. A [line set cover kit](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=mini+split+line+set+cover+kit&tag=fixitreal-20) protects the lines on the outside wall and makes the job look finished.
- **Seal the wall penetration** against water and pests.

If you decide a pre-charged system is right for you, these are sold as [DIY mini-split kits with pre-charged quick-connect lines](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=diy+mini+split+pre-charged+quick+connect+line+set&tag=fixitreal-20). Read the section above first, and check the line-set length before ordering.

## The electrical work

Hire an electrician. A mini-split needs a dedicated circuit and an outdoor disconnect. In the leading DIY line, the 9,000 and 12,000 BTU models run on 115V and the 18,000 and 24,000 BTU models need 230V. The leading DIY brand's own instructions say all electrical connections to a power source must be performed by a licensed electrician and comply with local codes. Ask your building department whether an electrical or mechanical permit applies. For what the visit should cost, see [electrician service call cost](/costs/electrician-service-call).

## Warranty

Read the warranty before you choose. Conventional units commonly require installation by a licensed HVAC technician for the warranty to apply. Kits in the DIY lines are sold with homeowner installation allowed, usually on condition that you register the product and follow the manual.

## When to hire the whole job out

- The system is a conventional (not pre-charged) unit
- It is a multi-zone system with several indoor heads
- The line run is longer than the pre-charged set you can buy
- The panel has no room for a new circuit
- You rent, or the building has rules about exterior equipment

In those cases get three written quotes, and confirm that the technician who will be on site holds EPA Section 608 certification.

## The one-line version

Mount it yourself, hire an electrician for the power, and treat the refrigerant connection as a decision to make with EPA's guidance in view, not just the box's.
