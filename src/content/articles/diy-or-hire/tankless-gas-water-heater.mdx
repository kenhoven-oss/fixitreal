A tankless gas water heater (also sold as an instant, on-demand or demand-type heater) looks like an easy swap. It is a box on a wall with two water pipes and a gas pipe. The verdict is still **hire a pro**, and the reason is not the plumbing.

<KeyTakeaway title="The short answer">
Hire a licensed installer for a gas tankless water heater. The gas line, the venting, the leak test and the permit are the job, and each one can hurt someone if it is wrong. Your part is choosing the unit, comparing quotes, and doing the yearly descaling afterwards.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Why this is not a DIY job

**Gas demand.** A standard tank water heater draws 30,000–40,000 BTU per hour. A whole-house tankless unit draws 150,000–200,000. In a retrofit that often means replacing an older 1/2-inch gas line with 3/4-inch. A unit starved of gas runs badly and can short-cycle the burner. Sizing gas pipe for the whole house, not just this appliance, is a licensed trade's calculation.

**Venting.** A tankless unit does not vent into the flue the old tank used. It needs its own venting, in the material and length the manufacturer specifies, usually run to an outside wall. Venting mistakes put combustion gases, including carbon monoxide, into the house.

**The leak test.** The U.S. Department of Energy's Building America checklist for these installs says the gas line shall be tested for leaks once installation is complete. That is a test with the right equipment, not a sniff.

**The other connections.** Even a gas unit needs a 120V supply for ignition and controls. High-efficiency (condensing) models need a condensate drain. In hard-water areas the unit may need a softener or scale protection ahead of it.

**The permit.** Water heater replacement needs a permit almost everywhere, and a fuel change or new gas line makes that more certain, not less. See what your city charges in our [water heater permit fees by city](/costs/water-heater-permit-cost-by-city) table.

**The warranty.** Check the warranty before you buy. Rinnai's installation manual states that installation and service must be performed by a licensed professional, and its warranty documents say improper installation may void coverage. Other brands have their own terms.

The U.S. Department of Energy puts it plainly: proper installation depends on many factors, so it is best to have a qualified plumbing and heating contractor install a demand water heater.

## What you can do yourself

- **Decide whether tankless is right for the house.** For many 2–3 person homes a tank is the better value. Run the numbers in our [tankless vs tank guide](/advice/tankless-vs-tank-water-heater) and the [four-way cost comparison](/costs/water-heater-cost-comparison).
- **Test your water hardness before you get quotes.** Hard water is what shortens a tankless unit's life. A pack of [water hardness test strips](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=water+hardness+test+strips&tag=fixitreal-20) tells you whether to ask installers about scale protection.
- **Clear the wall and the path.** Empty the area around the old tank and the route to the outside wall. Less time on site is a smaller bill.
- **Ask for service valves in the quote.** Isolation valves on the hot and cold lines are what make the yearly flush possible. Without them, descaling means a service call.
- **Do the yearly descaling.** With service valves fitted, flushing the heat exchanger is the one maintenance task most owners can take on. A [tankless water heater flush kit](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=tankless+water+heater+flush+kit&tag=fixitreal-20) is a small pump, two hoses and a bucket. Follow the steps in your model's manual, and stop at anything that involves the gas valve, burner or vent.
- **Put a carbon monoxide alarm nearby.** Any fuel-burning appliance warrants one. See our [carbon monoxide detector picks](/tools/best-carbon-monoxide-detectors).

## What a pro install costs

A whole-house gas tankless install typically runs **$1,500–$4,500**, and more when the gas line, venting and electrical all need work. The full breakdown, including the tank options, is in our [water heater replacement cost guide](/costs/water-heater-replacement).

Before you sign, the quote should say:

- Make, model and BTU rating of the unit
- Whether the gas line is being upsized, and to what size
- Venting material and where it terminates
- Whether service (isolation) valves are included
- Who pulls the permit, and that the fee is in the price
- That the gas line will be leak-tested and the install inspected

## What about an electric tankless unit?

No gas and no venting, but a different problem. A whole-house electric tankless unit adds a very large electrical load, often needing a panel that can carry 100 amps or more for it, and frequently a service upgrade. That is electrician work, and the panel upgrade can cost more than the heater.

## The one-line version

Choose it, prepare for it and maintain it yourself. Have a licensed installer connect the gas, the vent and the permit.
