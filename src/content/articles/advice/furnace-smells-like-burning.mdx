The first cold night of the year, the furnace kicks on, and the house smells like something's burning. Nine times out of ten it's dust that settled on the heat exchanger and burner assembly over the summer, and it's gone in an hour. The tenth time it's an electrical component, melting plastic, or gas — and those need the furnace off now. The smell itself tells you which one you have.

<QuickAnswer>
**Dusty, slightly acrid, first heat of the season, fading within an hour or two:** normal. Open windows, let it burn off. **Persistent burning after a day, or every cycle:** shut down and check the filter — a clogged filter overheats the unit. **Electrical (hot wire, ozone, "fishy"), plastic, or rubber:** shut off at the furnace switch **and** the breaker, don't run it until an HVAC tech has looked. **Rotten egg / sulfur:** that's gas — leave the house, call the utility from outside. Expect **$75–$150 for a diagnostic visit**, $150–$500 for the common repairs.
</QuickAnswer>

## Match the smell

| Smell | Likely cause | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dusty, like a hot iron on lint, first run of the season | Summer dust burning off the heat exchanger and burners | Normal. Ventilate; should fade in 1–2 hours and be gone within a day. |
| Same dusty smell, but persistent or every cycle | Clogged filter, or dirt in the blower / ducts | Replace the filter; if it persists after 2–3 cycles with a clean filter, schedule a cleaning. |
| Hot electrical, ozone, "fishy" | Overheating motor, failing capacitor or control board, wire insulation | **Shut off at switch and breaker.** Service call before running again. |
| Burning plastic or rubber | Foreign object in a duct or on the heat exchanger; belt or component failure | **Shut off.** Check registers and the blower compartment for objects; if nothing, service call. |
| Rotten egg / sulfur | Natural gas or propane leak | **Leave the house.** Call the gas utility from outside. [Gas smell sequence here.](/emergency-repairs/gas-smell-in-house) |
| Musty, dirty-sock | Mold or bacteria on the evaporator coil or in ducts | Not an emergency. Coil cleaning ($100–$300) or duct evaluation. |
| Smoky, like a fireplace | Cracked heat exchanger or backdrafting flue | **Shut off.** Verify CO detectors work. Service call — this is a safety issue. |

## The normal case: first heat of the season

Dust settles on the heat exchanger, burners, and blower housing over the months the furnace sits idle. The first time the burners light, that dust scorches. The smell is distinct — dry, a little sharp, sometimes described as "hot lint" — and it drifts through the registers with the first few cycles.

What normal looks like: strongest on the first cycle, fading over the next few, essentially gone within a day. Open windows for an hour, run the fan, replace the filter if it's due, and that's the entire response.

What's not normal: the smell getting stronger, coming back at full strength every cycle after the first day, or any of the electrical / plastic / gas notes above.

## The clogged-filter case

A filter loaded with summer dust restricts airflow. Less air moving across the heat exchanger means the exchanger runs hotter than designed; the furnace's high-limit switch may shut the burners off and restart them, and everything inside runs hot enough to keep that scorched smell going. It's the most common reason a "first-of-season" smell doesn't go away.

Pull the filter. If you can't see light through it, [replace it](/advice/how-often-to-change-air-filter) and give the furnace a few cycles. Keep the next filter's replacement date on a calendar — a $15 filter every 60–90 days in heating season prevents most of the "furnace smells weird" calls a tech gets.

<SafetyWarning>
Electrical and plastic smells mean shut it down at **both** the furnace's power switch (the light-switch-style box on or near the unit) and the breaker. A furnace with a failing motor or scorched wiring can keep cycling and get worse. If you ever see smoke from the unit or the registers, leave and call 911. And with any furnace smell that doesn't resolve, confirm your [CO detectors](/tools/best-carbon-monoxide-detectors) are working — a combustion or heat-exchanger problem is the classic CO source, and CO has no smell at all.
</SafetyWarning>

## What the repair costs

- **Diagnostic visit:** $75–$150, often credited toward repair.
- **Blower motor cleaning / duct cleaning:** $100–$300 / $300–$600.
- **Capacitor:** $100–$250. **Blower motor:** $300–$700. **Control board:** $300–$700.
- **Foreign object removal:** usually within the diagnostic fee.
- **Cracked heat exchanger:** on a furnace past ~12–15 years, this generally means [replacement, $4,000–$8,000](/costs/furnace-replacement-cost). Ask for the crack on a borescope photo or the combustion-analyzer numbers before you agree — "trust me, it's cracked" without evidence deserves a [second opinion](/advice/three-contractor-quotes).

<KensTake>
The pre-season furnace check is worth doing yourself in September: new filter, vacuum the blower compartment, run the heat for 20 minutes on a mild day with the windows open. You get the dust smell over with on your schedule instead of on the first freezing night, and if there's an electrical or gas smell you find out while every HVAC company in town still has appointments available.
</KensTake>
