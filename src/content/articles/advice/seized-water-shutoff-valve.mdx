The day you find out a shutoff valve will not turn is rarely a convenient day. It is the day a supply line is spraying, a toilet fill valve is overflowing, or a plumber needs to isolate a section of pipe. Testing valves while nothing is wrong costs a few minutes and a small risk; discovering they are seized in an emergency costs far more. This page covers how to test, what to leave alone, and when to replace.

## Know which valve is which

There are three levels.

**Fixture shutoffs** are the small stops under sinks and behind toilets. They isolate one fixture. Many are multi-turn stop valves with a small oval or round handle; newer ones are quarter-turn.

**Zone or branch valves** are on pipes near the water heater, in a utility area, or in a basement. They isolate part of the house. See [the valves near your water heater](/what-is-this/valve-near-water-heater) for how to identify them.

**The main shutoff** is where the water line enters the house, usually in a basement, crawl space, utility room, or garage, or at a meter outside. This is the one that matters in a real emergency.

## How to test a valve without making it worse

1. **Be ready for a drip.** Have a towel and a bucket nearby, and know where the main shutoff is before testing any lesser valve.
2. **Close it slowly.** Turn a round handle clockwise, or a lever a quarter turn, with steady hand pressure. If it moves, it is working. Then open it fully again.
3. **Check for weeping.** Dry the valve with a towel and watch the stem and packing nut for a minute. A drop at the stem after moving it means the packing is worn.
4. **Stop if it resists.** If it will not move with moderate hand pressure, do not add leverage. Note it for a plumber.

## What not to do

- **Do not use a cheater bar or large wrench on a stuck handle.** Old stems snap, and old handles strip.
- **Do not hammer the valve body or the pipe.** Vibration can loosen solder joints or fittings upstream.
- **Do not tighten the packing nut hard to stop a drip while the valve is stuck.** That can make the valve harder to turn without fixing the leak.
- **Do not try to replace a main shutoff yourself without a way to shut the water off upstream.** If the only way to stop the water is the valve you are removing, you need the utility or a plumber.

## What is worth trying on a fixture shutoff

A small amount of penetrating oil on the stem where it meets the packing nut, left for some time, followed by gentle back-and-forth movement, can free a valve that has just gone stiff. If it moves, work it open and closed a few times and leave it. If it does not, or it begins leaking at the stem, plan a replacement.

## Replacing a fixture shutoff

Under-sink and toilet stops are within reach of many homeowners, but only when the water can be shut off upstream. That usually means a working valve elsewhere or the main. Replace like with like in connection type (compression, solder, or push-fit), and use a new supply line rather than reusing the old one. If the old stop will not hold, or the pipe it is attached to turns, call a plumber.

## The main shutoff is a plumber job to fix

A gate valve at the main that will not move, or leaks when moved, is usually replaced with a ball valve. The work involves shutting the water off at the street or meter, draining the line, and cutting and re-connecting pipe, and doing it wrong can mean a leak in a wall or slab. Some utilities require their own approval to touch the shutoff at the meter. ## If you cannot close anything in an emergency

1. Turn off the water heater and the electric or gas supply to it if water is near it.
2. Close any fixture stops you can reach.
3. Close the main if it works; if it does not, call the water utility, which can shut off the supply at the street, and call a plumber.
4. Move belongings and electronics out of the way, and see [water leak detection](/advice/water-leak-detection) for tracing the source.

## Bottom line

Test your shutoffs once or twice a year, starting with the main. If one moves, you are done. If one is stiff, free it gently or replace it. If one will not move at all, do not force it: schedule a replacement while nothing is leaking, so you have a working way to stop water on the day you need one.
