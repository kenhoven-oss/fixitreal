You pull the knob on the tub spout to send water to the shower, and half of it keeps pouring out of the spout. Or the water only reaches the showerhead as a weak spray. Either way, the diverter is not doing its one job. This page explains which kind you have, what usually fails, and what the fix looks like for each.

## Which diverter do you have?

There are three common setups, and the fix depends on which.

**A pull-up diverter in the tub spout.** A knob on top of the spout lifts a plug that blocks the spout and pushes water up the shower pipe. This is the most common type, and the most common one to wear out.

**A diverter built into the faucet valve.** On two- and three-handle systems, a third handle (or a push-pull on a single-handle valve) switches between tub and shower. A failure here is inside the valve, not in the spout.

**A diverter that is only a button on the tub spout.** Some spouts have a push-down ring or button instead of a knob.

If you can see a knob on the spout itself, you have the first type. If the switching is done with a handle on the wall, it is in the valve.

## What usually fails

On a spout diverter, the plug or its seal wears down, or mineral buildup keeps it from seating. The giveaway is **water coming from the spout while the shower is running**. On a valve diverter, the cartridge or stem seals wear and the switching handle may be loose, hard to turn, or leak at the wall.

A spout that leaks where it meets the wall is a different problem. If water is running behind or around the spout, see [shower caulk failing](/advice/shower-caulk-failing) and check for wet drywall; a spout that is loose on the pipe can let water into the wall.

## Fixing a worn spout diverter

The usual fix is to replace the whole spout, because the diverter is part of it and the spouts are not expensive.

1. **Identify the mounting.** Look underneath the spout, near the wall, for a small hex screw: that is a slip-on spout. If you find no screw, the spout is threaded; you can unscrew it by hand or with a pipe wrench padded with a rag, turning counterclockwise.
2. **Remove it.** Loosen the set screw and pull the spout straight off, or unscrew it.
3. **Check the pipe stub.** Look at the short pipe sticking out of the wall. A threaded stub means a threaded spout; a smooth copper stub means a slip-on spout. Buy a replacement of the same type, and measure the length from the wall to the end of the pipe, because slip-on spouts come in lengths.
4. **Install the new one.** Follow the package instructions. Do not overtighten a set screw, and use plumber's tape on threaded spouts only if the package says to.
5. **Seal the wall joint.** Run a thin bead of caulk along the top and sides where the spout meets the wall, leaving the bottom open so any water inside can drain out.

If the old spout will not budge or the pipe turns in the wall when you twist, stop. A pipe that moves can break a joint inside the wall.

## When the problem is in the valve

If the switching handle is on the wall, a worn cartridge or diverter stem is the likely cause. Replacing it means shutting off the water, removing the handle and trim, and pulling the cartridge, which needs the right replacement for your specific valve brand and model. If you do not know the brand, take photos of the handle and trim before disassembly. Many homeowners choose to hire this one; see [plumber service-call costs](/costs/plumber-service-call) for how trip fees and hourly rates are normally billed.

## When to call a plumber

- The spout or pipe moves in the wall when you try to remove it.
- Water appears at the wall, ceiling below, or around the tub after use. See [water leak detection](/advice/water-leak-detection).
- The diverter is in the valve and you cannot identify the brand or find the right cartridge.
- Hot and cold behavior changed along with the diverter problem.

## If it shows up on an inspection report

Inspectors often write this up as *"diverter does not function properly, water continues to flow from the tub spout while the shower is on."* It is a repair item rather than a safety item, and the spout replacement is a small job. Treat it as low priority unless water is getting into the wall. See the [sample home inspection report walkthrough](/resources/sample-home-inspection-report) for how items like this sit among the rest of a typical report.

## Bottom line

A tub diverter that will not divert is almost always a worn plug or seal. Check where the switch is, replace the spout if the diverter is in the spout, and call a plumber if the problem is in the valve or the pipe will not hold still. Fix it before it becomes a wall leak.
