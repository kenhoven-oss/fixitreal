The pilot lights fine while you hold the knob down. Let go, and it dies. Do it again, same thing. This is the single most common gas water heater complaint, and it has one overwhelmingly likely cause: the thermocouple. It's a $15 part, and on most tanks it's a 20-minute job.

<QuickAnswer>
**Lights while you hold the button, dies when you release:** the thermocouple is worn or misaligned — **replace it ($10–$20 part, 20–30 minutes DIY, or $150–$300 for a plumber).** **Stays lit for minutes or hours, then goes out:** dirty pilot orifice, drafts, or a failing gas valve. **Won't light at all:** no gas — check the supply valve, purge air by holding the button longer, or the orifice is clogged. **You smell gas:** stop, leave, [call the utility from outside](/emergency-repairs/gas-smell-in-house). On a tank over 10–12 years old with a failing gas valve, [replacement](/costs/water-heater-replacement) usually beats repair.
</QuickAnswer>

## Read the failure pattern

| What happens | Most likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lights, dies as soon as you let go of the knob | Thermocouple worn, loose at the valve, or not in the flame | Replace or reseat the thermocouple |
| Lights, holds for 30–60 seconds, then dies | Weak thermocouple, or pilot flame too small (dirty orifice) | Clean the pilot orifice; replace thermocouple if it continues |
| Stays lit for hours or days, then goes out | Drafts across the burner area, dirty flame sensor, intermittent gas valve | Check for drafts and blocked vents; if random, gas valve is failing |
| Won't light at all | Gas supply off, air in the line, clogged orifice | Confirm the valve, hold the pilot button 60–90 seconds, clean the orifice |
| Lights, but burner never fires after the pilot | Thermostat / gas valve failure | Gas valve replacement, or replace the tank |
| Pilot flame is yellow and lazy, not blue and steady | Dirty orifice or restricted combustion air | Clean the orifice, clear the air intake screen |

## The thermocouple fix

1. Turn the gas control knob to **Off** and wait five minutes for any gas to clear.
2. Locate the thermocouple: a thin copper tube running from the gas control valve to a bracket beside the pilot, with a probe tip that sits in the pilot flame.
3. Unscrew the fitting at the gas valve (usually a 7/16" or 3/8" nut) and pull the probe from its bracket.
4. Install the new one — same routing, probe tip positioned so the pilot flame envelops the top 3/8 inch of it. Thread the nut finger-tight plus a quarter turn; overtightening damages the fitting.
5. Relight following the instructions on the tank label: knob to Pilot, hold, light, keep holding 30–60 seconds, release, turn to On.

Bring the old part to the store or match the length; universal thermocouples in 18, 24, and 36 inch lengths cover most tanks. If your heater is a newer sealed-chamber (FVIR) design, you'll likely need the brand-specific pilot assembly instead — same idea, $40–$80.

While you're in there, look at the pilot flame. It should be a steady blue cone. A weak yellow flame means the pilot orifice is dirty — a puff of compressed air or a careful pass with a thin wire clears it — and a dirty orifice will kill a brand-new thermocouple's performance too.

<SafetyWarning>
If you smell gas at any point — before, during, or after relighting — stop. Don't keep trying to light it. Turn the knob to Off, leave, and call the gas utility from outside. Never use a match or lighter to search for the smell, and don't run the pilot-light button for more than 90 seconds at a time. A water heater in a small closet also needs its combustion air: if the room is sealed tight, a pilot that keeps dying can be starving for air, and that's a [carbon monoxide](/emergency-repairs/carbon-monoxide-alarm-going-off) risk as well as a nuisance.
</SafetyWarning>

## When to stop and call

- **You've replaced the thermocouple and it still won't hold** — the gas control valve is likely failing. That's a $250–$500 repair, and on a tank past 10 years it's the moment to price [replacement](/costs/water-heater-replacement) instead.
- **The pilot goes out randomly every few days** with no draft explanation — also usually the gas valve.
- **There's water at the base of the tank** — a [leaking tank](/advice/water-heater-leaking) isn't repairable, and relighting the pilot on a failing tank is wasted effort.
- **Any smell of gas, scorching around the burner access, or soot** — that's a combustion problem, not a pilot problem.

<KensTake>
Plumbers charge $150–$300 to do this, and most of that is the trip. If you can turn a wrench and follow the label on the tank, the thermocouple is the most satisfying DIY fix in the house: cheap part, obvious symptom, immediate result. The one rule is the gas rule — smell it, stop. Everything else about this job is forgiving.
</KensTake>
