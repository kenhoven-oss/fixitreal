There is no national water heater permit fee. Each city or county sets its own, and many publish it. The table below lists the fee each city prints on its own fee schedule, word for word, with the date on that schedule and a link to the document. Your installer normally pulls the permit and passes the fee through in the quote.

## How to read the table

- **The fee is the city's wording, not ours.** Nothing here is averaged, rounded or estimated. Where a city charges a base fee plus a per-fixture or per-appliance fee, both parts are shown, because that is how your permit will be billed.
- **"Schedule dated" is the date the city prints on its fee schedule.** Some cities still publish schedules that are years old. An old date does not mean the fee is wrong, but it is worth a phone call before you rely on it.
- **The source link goes to the permitting authority's own page.** Fee schedules change. If the figure on the city's page differs from this table, the city's page is right.
- **Each city name opens that city's water heater cost page,** which adds who may pull the permit there and what local inspectors check.

## Who pays the permit fee

You do, through the quote. In most places the installer pulls the permit and lists the fee as a line item. When you compare bids, check that each one says whether the permit is included and who pulls it. A bid that leaves the permit out is not cheaper; it is missing a cost you will still owe.

For what the whole job should cost, permit included, see the [water heater replacement cost guide](/costs/water-heater-replacement).

## If your city is not in the table

The table only includes cities whose permitting authority publishes a fee for this job. If yours is missing, call the building or permit office and ask two questions: what the permit fee is for a like-for-like residential water heater replacement, and whether a homeowner may pull that permit or only a licensed contractor.
