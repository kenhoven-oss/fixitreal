<QuickAnswer>
A tank is cheapest to buy, gas is cheapest to run, and tankless lasts longest. Spread over the life of the unit, a gas tank and a gas tankless heater cost about the same per year, and both cost less than either electric type. Hard water tips the balance back toward a tank.
</QuickAnswer>

## The four types side by side

| Type | Installed cost | Energy per year | Typical lifespan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gas tank (50 gal) | $900–$2,500 | $250–$400 | 10–13 years |
| Electric tank (50 gal) | $1,250–$2,200 | $400–$600 | 10–13 years |
| Gas tankless (whole house) | $1,500–$4,500 | $200–$300 | 18–22 years |
| Electric tankless (whole house) | $1,800–$3,500 | $350–$500 | 18–22 years |

Installed costs are the same figures as our [water heater replacement cost guide](/costs/water-heater-replacement), which breaks each one into unit, labor, permit and code items. Energy and lifespan figures are the planning ranges from our [tankless vs tank guide](/advice/tankless-vs-tank-water-heater). Your own energy cost depends on local gas and electricity rates and how much hot water the household uses.

## Cost per year of service

Purchase price alone is misleading, because a tankless unit lasts nearly twice as long. This table divides the installed cost by the lifespan and adds the yearly energy cost.

| Type | Install cost per year of life | Energy per year | Total per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gas tank (50 gal) | $70–$250 | $250–$400 | **$320–$650** |
| Electric tank (50 gal) | $95–$220 | $400–$600 | **$495–$820** |
| Gas tankless | $70–$250 | $200–$300 | **$270–$550** |
| Electric tankless | $80–$195 | $350–$500 | **$430–$695** |

How the table is built: the low end is the lowest installed cost spread over the longest lifespan, plus the lowest energy figure; the high end is the reverse. It leaves out maintenance, which matters for tankless: in hard-water areas, budget $150–$300 a year for descaling if you pay someone to do it. Add that and gas tankless moves to roughly $420–$850 a year, above a gas tank.

## What the numbers mean

- **With gas service and soft or treated water,** gas tankless and a gas tank are close. Choose on capacity and how long you will stay, not on running cost.
- **With gas service and hard water,** a gas tank is usually the cheaper choice unless you descale the tankless unit yourself.
- **With no gas service,** both electric options cost more per year than gas. Before choosing between them, price a heat-pump water heater, which uses far less electricity than a standard electric tank.
- **If the budget is tight this week,** a like-for-like tank is the lowest bill today, and that is a legitimate reason to choose it.

## Costs the table does not show

- **Switching type.** Moving from tank to tankless, or from electric to gas, adds gas-line, venting or electrical work on top of the figures above.
- **Electric tankless and the panel.** A whole-house unit often needs 100 amps or more of spare capacity. A service upgrade is a separate, larger job.
- **Permit.** Required almost everywhere. See [water heater permit fees by city](/costs/water-heater-permit-cost-by-city).
- **Rebates.** Utility rebates vary by area and change often; confirm them in writing before counting on one.

## Which one to get quotes on

Get quotes on the type you already have first, because like-for-like is the baseline every other option should be compared against. Then price one alternative. If that alternative is a gas tankless unit, read [why it is a hire-a-pro job](/diy-or-hire/tankless-gas-water-heater) before you compare bids.
