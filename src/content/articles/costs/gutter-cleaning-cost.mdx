Gutter cleaning is priced by risk and reach more than by dirt. The same 180 feet of gutter costs $120 on a one-story ranch and $300 on a two-story with a steep roof, because the second job is a ladder-and-fall-protection job and the first is a step-stool job. Here's how the pricing actually breaks down, and what a fair quote looks like this fall.

## What you're paying for

| House | Typical cost | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-story, 150–200 ft of gutter | $90–$200 | Ladder work at 10–12 feet; 1–2 hours |
| Two-story, 200–300 ft | $150–$325 | Extension ladder or roof access; 2–3 hours; fall-protection setup |
| Three-story or steep/complex roof | $300–$500+ | Lift or harness work; often a two-person crew |
| Add-on: downspout unclogging | $30–$75 per clog | Snaking or disassembling a downspout |
| Add-on: gutter guard removal/reinstall | $50–$150 | Depends on guard type |
| Add-on: minor repairs (reseal a seam, rehang a section) | $75–$200 | Often done on the same visit at a discount |

The pricing drivers, in order: **stories** (height is labor and liability), **linear feet**, **roof pitch and access** (a walkable 4:12 roof versus a 10:12 that requires ladder-only access), **debris load** (a house under oaks after a wet fall can double the time), and **downspout condition** — a clogged downspout that has to be taken apart adds real time.

## Fair quote, or padded?

Two things to push back on:

- **Per-foot pricing on a small house.** $2 per foot sounds cheap until it's applied to a 90-foot bungalow with a $150 trip minimum. Ask for the total.
- **"You need new gutters" from the cleaning crew.** Sagging, separated seams, and rust spots are real, but they're usually $75–$300 in repairs, not $1,500–$4,000 in replacement. Get the specific defect in writing and [a second opinion](/advice/three-contractor-quotes) before agreeing to replace.

A good sign: the company flushes the downspouts with a hose as part of the job and tells you which ones drained slowly. A cleaning that leaves a clogged downspout hasn't done the part that matters.

## DIY: when it's fine and when it isn't

On a **one-story house**, gutter cleaning is a reasonable DIY job: a stable ladder, gloves, a scoop, a bucket on a hook, and a hose to flush the downspouts. Budget two hours and $30 in tools you'll reuse. Gutter-cleaning attachments for a [wet/dry shop vac](/tools/best-shop-vacs-for-water-cleanup) or a leaf blower work well on dry debris from the ground, and keep you off the ladder entirely.

On a **two-story house**, the honest answer is that the $200 you save is the price of the risk. Ladder falls from over 10 feet are among the most common serious home-maintenance injuries, and two-story gutter work means moving a 24-foot extension ladder repeatedly on uneven ground while leaning. If you do it: never lean sideways from a ladder, keep your belt buckle between the rails, and have a second person foot the ladder. If any of that sounds like more than you want to manage, $200 twice a year is a bargain.
